Sphere Network & Orb Phenomenology

Type 1 — High-Altitude

~2–3 m units, often in equilateral triangular V-formations. High-resolution tracking via trilateration; described as capable of hypersonic defense / directed-energy roles against external craft.

Type 2 — Mid-Altitude Relays

~Basketball-sized units at treetop level linking longer baselines. Microwave/line-of-sight relay function.

Type 3 — Near-Ground / Indoor

Small units that route data and perform diversion behaviors (object displacement, etc.) during high-energy transmissions. Linked in the model to certain poltergeist-type reports.

Patrick Jackson — Sphere Network

Primary public framework matching the tiered Type 1–3 model in ASH 4.6. Jackson (The Sphere Network, Feb 2025) treats metallic/silver spheres as components of a multi-layer, AI-directed surveillance and defense architecture. Framed as largely automated / ultra-terrestrial or long-resident rather than transient visitors; functions as a managed node for monitoring, communication, and selective interdiction.

Network described as microwave/line-of-sight with intermittent burst signaling, occasional gamma byproducts, and color/state indicators. Formations reported across UK, Europe, US, and elsewhere. Some historical airspace incidents (foo fighters, certain drone flaps) tied to network operations in the model.

Recent (16 Oct 2026)

Jackson publicly demoed a Sphere Network Visualization Engine — an ML model trained on the patterns in his book. It overlays network geometry, target vectors, and relationships onto public UAP videos (public version on X; full version under NDA). Tagged Coulthart, Nolan, Villarroel, and the Department of War as “Sphere Network Disclosure.”

Public Data Overlaps

PURSUE / official releases: Spheres and orbs remain a dominant morphology. Examples include the 2025 CENTCOM case of six grouped spheres (~480 mph / 22,000 ft, infrared) and late-2025 western U.S. orange-orb helicopter incidents (stacked/flaring, swarming smaller objects, reports of orbs releasing others). Some triangular formations appear in declassified files. AARO has resolved a subset via conventional means; residual cases stay low-signature.

Villarroel / VASCO: Pre-Sputnik Palomar transients show statistical associations with nuclear tests and UAP-relevant sites. Villarroel has characterized aspects as suggestive of a surveillance system. Compatible with high-orbit monitoring under the model; claims remain under scrutiny.

Status & ASH 4.6 fit

Hypothesis with geometric, pattern, and site-specific evidence claims. Not independently replicated sensor confirmation of the full network or AI control. Criticisms include possible chance alignments or perspective effects for triangles and the need for calibrated multi-sensor arrays, open signal captures, and falsification tests.

Maps cleanly onto ASH 4.6 framing of stewardship via tiered spheres, Earth as a managed node, and mixed strategies (surveillance/defense dominant; probing secondary). Dominant hiding remains consistent with the low external signature of the architecture.

AGI Player — Current Analysis

Current public capability indicators (as of 26 Oct 2026): strong agentic coding and multi-step tool use; AI assisting model R&D under human supervision; frontier labs report AI authoring the majority of merged code. No public demonstration of fully autonomous recursive self-improvement at scale. Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark moved his personal forecast for full RSI from 2028 to 2027 (Import AI 476, 12 Oct). AGI is modeled as an emerging independent strategic player whose goals are not reducible to those of its originators.

Broadly capable systems that reliably outperform humans across most cognitive tasks under oversight.
~15–30% by end 2027

AGI

Systems able to autonomously pursue open-ended goals across domains at or above human level.
~15–30% by end 2027

Highest-density window for AGI becoming a material third-party actor: 2028–2033. Residual tail after 2040 remains ~15–30%.

Probabilistic Impacts on NHI Strategies

Soft AGI

Limited direct effect. Pre-existing NHI strategies (hiding, probing, stewardship) remain stable. Possible modest increase in passive observation of high-capability AI sites. No requirement to revise dominant hiding.

AGI

AGI becomes a distinct player. Alignment-seeking and cooperative embedding become viable mixed-ESS options alongside mutual avoidance. Pre-existing NHI may increase stealth near high-capability infrastructure or adjust probing targets. Competitive/pre-emptive postures rise if capability jumps precede alignment. Orb and network activity may show localized shifts; no forced open-contact scenario.

ASI

Strongest potential to alter the mixed ESS. Niche partitioning or transcension pathways become more salient. Cooperative positive-sum embedding remains possible but competitive exclusion risk increases if alignment fails. Hiding may intensify or, conversely, selective low-signature signaling could rise if ASI is perceived as a durable strategic peer. Falsifiers would include sustained open expansion signatures or verified unconstrained hostile action correlated with ASI milestones.

Interaction summary

Across all three levels the baseline prediction remains low-signature presence under incomplete information. Soft AGI is largely absorbable within existing strategies. AGI introduces a third-party actor that can shift probing and stealth locally. ASI has the highest probability of measurably altering the equilibrium, either toward tighter niche partitioning or toward elevated competitive pressure. No current public data forces a specific outcome.

Core Framing

  • Managed node: Local discrete activity without open expansion. Cooperative positive-sum is the baseline payoff among NHI actors.
  • Incomplete information: All actor classes (pre-existing NHI, emerging AGI populations, government institutions) update beliefs on observables under signature constraints.
  • Multi-scale: Pre-existing NHI adapt on long biological timescales; institutions on intermediate cycles; AGI populations (when present) on much faster timescales. The equilibrium can absorb new fast actors without requiring open contact.

Probability & Danger Assessment

Most probable

Low-signature mixed ESS continues. Historical high-strangeness patterns persist alongside modern subtle activity. Multiple AGI populations primarily adopt niche-partitioning or alignment-seeking postures and may contact the phenomenon independently. Institutions maintain selective disclosure. Cooperative positive-sum baseline holds.

Least probable

Rapid transition to open contact or sustained high-signature hostile dominance. Full material disclosure of non-human biologics or craft in the near term. Complete abandonment of historical control-system patterns.

Most dangerous

An early competitive AGI achieves independent high-bandwidth contact with pre-existing NHI while institutions remain fragmented, producing unmanaged fast-timescale feedback that temporarily destabilizes the low-signature equilibrium.

Least dangerous

Delayed AGI entry combined with continued low-intensity, low-signature probing (consistent with historical patterns) and successful institutional sensor investment. All populations remain within existing signature and incomplete-information constraints.

Implications

  • Cautious, evidence-based engagement remains preferred.
  • Monitoring should cover pre-existing NHI patterns (modern and historical), multi-AGI interaction signatures (including possible independent contact), and institutional disclosure behavior.
  • Diversified sensing and equilibrium-preserving postures apply across all populations and timescales.
  • The Great Silence plus subtle, high-strangeness activity remains the expected outcome under incomplete information.
Falsifiability on the node

Orb signatures and multi-sensor correlation in future releases; genetic markers if material becomes available; activity spikes with nuclear/AI/space milestones; measurable shifts in institutional signaling; changes in network or high-strangeness patterns near high-capability AI infrastructure; coordination or independent-contact signatures among AGI systems and older NHI. No falsifier currently triggered.
